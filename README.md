# HR_Employee_Analytics
This project analyzes a synthetic employee dataset to understand employee attrition, workforce composition, compensation, satisfaction, overtime and tenure.
overtime and tenure.
Business Problem
The organization wants to understand:
Which departments have the highest attrition?
Which roles have higher retention risk?
Does overtime appear associated with attrition?
How does attrition vary by tenure?
What workforce KPIs should HR monitor?
Where should retention efforts be prioritized?
Objectives
Calculate HR KPIs.
Measure overall attrition.
Compare departments and roles.
Analyze tenure and age groups.
Investigate overtime and satisfaction.
Compare compensation.
Produce actionable HR recommendations.

Key KPIs
KPI	Definition
Total Employees	Count of employees
Attrition Count	Employees with Attrition = Yes
Attrition Rate	Attrition Count ÷ Total Employees
Average Monthly Income	Mean monthly income
Average Tenure	Mean years at company
Average Job Satisfaction	Mean score from 1–5
Overtime Rate	Employees working overtime ÷ total employees
Dataset Results
The included synthetic dataset contains 850 employees.
Overall attrition rate: 16.4%
Attrition count: 139
Average monthly income: ₹69,122
Average tenure: 3.4 years
Average job satisfaction: 3.33/5
Overtime rate: 28.4%
Highest departmental attrition: IT (18.4%)
Business Recommendations
Investigate workload and employee experience in high-attrition departments.
Monitor overtime as a potential retention-risk indicator.
Strengthen career development, recognition and promotion pathways.
Compare attrition by role, tenure, satisfaction and compensation.
Build an early-warning dashboard using leading indicators.
Analytical Limitation
Association does not prove causation. For example, if overtime employees show higher attrition, this does not prove overtime causes employees to leave. Other factors may contribute.
